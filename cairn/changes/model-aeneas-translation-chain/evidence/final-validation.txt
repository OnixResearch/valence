## Mechanism selection and audit

Three mechanisms were evaluated. Extending `formal_proof_chain` with a closed
provider submodule survived because it preserves one Valence proof-linkage
authority while enforcing Aeneas-specific roles and edges. Dispatch through the
generic evidence-chain adapter was rejected because its open kind projection
cannot prove the required stage ordering or promotion policy. A parallel
Aeneas evidence authority was rejected because it would duplicate and weaken
the formal-proof chain boundary.

The adversarial source audit found and repaired duplicate artifact-role aliasing,
"at least one" proof-card/provenance matches, and unclosed non-function manifest
sections. The final validator rejects duplicate artifact locators, requires one
matching Aeneas proof card and one receipt provenance record, and binds function,
type, global, trait-declaration, and trait-implementation inventory counts.
VibeThinker was attempted once as an advisory reviewer and returned `fetch
failed`; it is not counted as evidence.

## Focused positive and negative checks

```text
$ cargo test -p valence-core --test aeneas_translation_chain -- --nocapture
5 passed; 0 failed
```

The five tests cover the frozen positive chain; a typed 37-case negative
matrix; exhaustive removal/substitution/promotion/tamper loops over every node,
required edge, digest domain, intermediate role, and loaded artifact; canonical
ordering/path/label/run-local-ID laws; and proof-relevant identity changes.
Invalid chains never emit a passing generic handoff.

## Non-claims

These checks establish canonical BLAKE3 identity, exact typed linkage, bounded
role policy, and required non-claim conformance only. They do not establish Rust
correctness; Charon, Aeneas, Lean, translation, external-model, theorem,
adequacy-policy, requirement-selection, or verifier soundness; semantic
equivalence; requirement satisfaction; whole-program correctness; lifecycle
readiness; or release eligibility.
